# Draft Manuscript Section: Multiscale Modeling of Mechanically Structured Superconductivity

## 3.x Multiscale Modeling of Mechanically Structured Superconductivity

The six-device series indicates that the superconducting response cannot be organized by nominal film force alone. Devices with related nominal stressor conditions occupy different transport regimes, while the strongest structured-connectivity signatures occur in devices with clear spatial mechanical discontinuities, including the cracked AS005 device and the half-covered AS006 device. We therefore used a geometry-aware superconducting-network model to test whether normalized mechanical heterogeneity supplies transport-relevant spatial information beyond a scalar stressor descriptor.

The modeling workflow was deliberately constrained. The transport architecture, evidence hierarchy, device labels, and downstream scoring rules were fixed before introducing the final mechanics prior. The mechanics calculation was used only to supply a normalized spatial prior, not to retune device-specific superconducting parameters. This distinction is central to the interpretation below.

## 3.x.1 Geometry-Aware Superconducting Network

The transport model represents each device as a two-dimensional superconducting network. The model separates local superconducting strength from connectivity: a local transition-scale field describes where superconductivity is favored, while weak-link transparencies describe whether superconducting regions form effective current-carrying paths. Four-probe transport is then evaluated through the resulting network response.

This separation is essential because several experimental signatures are naturally connectivity-like rather than purely local-Tc-like: residual resistance, broadened or multi-scale transitions, current redistribution, field-sensitive nonlinear response, and the exceptional behavior of devices with cracks or stressor boundaries. The final modeling question is therefore not simply whether mechanics shifts a local transition temperature, but whether mechanical heterogeneity helps organize the superconducting network.

All mechanics-prior tests below preserve the frozen transport architecture. No device-specific retuning, weak-link reoptimization, new nuisance parameter, or retrospective reclassification of the six-device hierarchy is introduced.

## 3.x.2 Normalized Forward-Mechanics Prior

Forward mechanics calculations were used to construct a normalized mechanics-derived localization prior, denoted here as H_gradient(x,y). This field summarizes spatial mechanical-gradient structure generated by the measured or inferred device geometry. In the final model, H_gradient(x,y) is treated as a normalized spatial prior rather than as a measured strain tensor.

The distinction is important. The calculations do not reconstruct epsilon_xx, epsilon_yy, and epsilon_xy in the measured devices, and no absolute strain value is used as a transport input. Instead, the model tests whether the spatial organization of the normalized mechanics-derived field carries information relevant to transport.

AS005 and AS006 provide complementary anchor cases. AS005 contains an accidental crack or discontinuity, while AS006 contains a designed half-coverage boundary. These devices have different physical origins but converge on the same model-level hypothesis: mechanical localization can organize weak-link connectivity in the superconducting network.

## 3.x.3 Mechanics-Prior Transport Test

To test whether H_gradient(x,y) contains transport-relevant spatial information, the mechanics prior was compared against three controls under frozen downstream transport rules: the existing geometry prior, a mean-matched uniform prior, and spatially randomized mechanics priors. This comparison isolates the upstream spatial prior as the changed ingredient.

The mechanics-derived prior improved the frozen transport description relative to the geometry-only and uniform controls. It also outperformed randomized fields that preserve prior statistics while destroying spatial registration. This result shows that the spatial organization of the mechanics-derived field matters, not merely its distribution of values.

The strongest support came from AS005 and AS006, the same devices that independently anchor the structured-connectivity interpretation. At the same time, AS001-AS003 retained their guard behavior, indicating that the mechanics prior did not indiscriminately impose structured superconductivity on every device.

## 3.x.4 Weak-Link Connectivity as the Dominant Supported Role

After establishing that the mechanics-derived field is transport relevant, we tested how that information enters the superconducting network. The final coupling-role ablation compared a local-Tc-only role, a weak-link/connectivity-only role, and a diagnostic both-channel role.

The weak-link/connectivity mapping retained the dominant explanatory benefit of the mechanics-informed model. The local-Tc-only mapping provided secondary support but did not account for the transport evidence as effectively. The diagnostic both-channel mapping was not promoted as the primary interpretation because it has greater interpretive freedom.

This result shifts the physical interpretation away from a simple scalar statement that mechanics locally raises Tc. The supported interpretation is more specific: normalized mechanical heterogeneity primarily reorganizes the weak-link connectivity of the two-dimensional superconducting network, while local transition-scale modulation provides a supporting contribution.

Current-weighted overlap metrics provide a spatial check on this interpretation. During the transition and low-temperature stages, the modeled current preferentially samples regions with high H_gradient relative to randomized controls, particularly in AS005 and AS006. This links the mechanics-derived localization field to transport-relevant current redistribution.

## 3.x.5 Robustness and Model Scope

The final robustness checks tested the conclusion against the main ways it could have been fragile: disorder realization, grid/coarse-graining, prior normalization, randomized current-overlap nulls, and dependence on a single anchor device. The weak-link/connectivity role remained preferred across disorder, grid, and normalization variants. The current-overlap signal for AS005 and AS006 remained far above randomized-field nulls, and either AS005 or AS006 independently supported the final interpretation when the other anchor was held out.

These checks close the model-development loop. The frozen conclusion is that mechanical-gradient structure is transport relevant, primarily through weak-link connectivity, with local Tc modulation as a secondary contribution. The calculations identify normalized transport-relevant mechanical structure; they do not reconstruct absolute strain in the measured devices and do not prove a microscopic pairing mechanism controlled directly by |grad epsilon|.

The central model is therefore complete. Further work should focus on publication figures and manuscript presentation. A targeted AS005/AS006 three-dimensional mechanics visualization may be useful as supplemental validation if it shows that the reduced-model localization feature persists in a layered 3D geometry, but it is not required to support the main transport conclusion.

## Compact Modeling Conclusion

A geometry-aware two-dimensional superconducting-network model was combined with a normalized forward-mechanics description of the device geometry. The mechanics-derived spatial prior improved the frozen transport description relative to geometry-only, uniform, and spatially randomized controls, with the strongest support obtained for AS005 and AS006. Coupling-role ablations showed that the dominant contribution of the mechanical prior was through weak-link connectivity, while modulation of the local superconducting transition scale provided a secondary contribution. These conclusions remained robust to disorder, numerical coarse-graining, prior normalization, randomized spatial controls, and anchor-device selection. The calculations identify transport-relevant mechanical heterogeneity but do not reconstruct the absolute strain tensor of the measured devices.
